# North Star Summit 2026 2027

George Hayward | Hayward Data Science | Prepared September 27, 2026

I propose a course that begins with students’ goals and spreadsheets, moves into Python and clear communication, and ends with a critical look at AI and final presentations in April. I will lead one live workshop each month. Students will work asynchronously between sessions with support from two school teachers.

**Class** Approximately six grade 12 students; no coding experience required. Six proposed 45-minute workshops and one 60-minute presentation workshop, October through April. Suggested workload: 30–60 minutes per active week, adjusted for testing and individual needs.

### Phase 1 Meet the data

Meet Mr. Hayward, set a goal, and learn your way around the spreadsheet. Try the optional bonuses if you want a stretch.

### Phase 2 Learn to code and explain

Answer questions in Python, write a work email, and make charts you can explain.

### Phase 3 Think with AI and present

Use AI to revisit your work, test its answers, and share what you learned. We finish in April.

### What students will learn

Use spreadsheet formulas and Python to reproduce results; filter data and choose useful statistics; make labeled charts; write a clear work email; verify AI-generated claims; and explain a finding, its evidence, a limitation and a next step.

### Work and feedback

Each student keeps one personal Google Sheet and one Colab notebook. Each assignment has an answer Form: students type their answers, submit the appropriate file link, then revise after feedback. Assessment emphasizes working formulas or code, accurate interpretation, clear communication and improvement. School grading policy remains with the school.

### A01 Explore weight data in Sheets

**Objective** Use COUNT, MEDIAN, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX and a conditional average to answer questions reproducibly.

**Student work** Open Mr. Hayward’s worked example, then your own Sheet. Use its Data tab to calculate count, median, mean, minimum, maximum and weekend average. Keep your formulas so someone else can follow them.

**Submit by Oct 22** Six spreadsheet results, formulas and your explanation. Submit your answers and Sheet link in the assignment Form.

**Check understanding** Your formulas refer to the Data tab. Your teachers can open your personal Sheet.

Optional extension: define and find a weight-gain streak; then use an exact-match VLOOKUP with a weekday mean table. The VLOOKUP exercise is a proposed reconstruction of the bonus described by George.

**Support** Teachers can work through one step aloud with a student who is stuck. Students ready for an extension can test a different statistic, chart choice or AI prompt and explain what changed.

## Tell the story with charts

**Workshop** Feb 11, 2027 | 45 minutes | tentative

**Live agenda** 5 min: choose a chart for a question. 10 min: demonstrate common histogram bins. 10 min: adapt the weekend chart. 10 min: plot actual dates and discuss the mean. 5 min: interpret a pattern. 5 min: submission check.

### A04 See the distribution

**Objective** Create a labeled histogram and compare groups using consistent bins and sample counts.

**Student work** Make the weekend chart, use common bins for comparison, and report the observation count in each group.

**Submit by Feb 18** A labeled weekend histogram and a short comparison with weekdays in the notebook.

**Check understanding** Chart title and axes identify the subset and units. Do not interpret fewer observations as lower weight.

### A05 Put time back into the story

**Student work** After the histogram, make a chart with real dates on the horizontal axis. Compare what it shows with the single overall mean.

**Submit by Feb 25** A dated time-series chart, computed overall mean, and an explanation linked to code.

**Check understanding** Date axis reflects real dates. Written claims name the relevant time period and avoid unsupported causes.

**Student work** Use the school-approved free AI tool available to you. Give it the public GitHub CSV or a teacher-provided excerpt. Ask it to improve one earlier analysis. Verify at least two numerical claims against your own Sheet or Python. Export to Docs/Sheets if available; otherwise paste the output into a Google Doc and label it AI-generated. Pause for March 22–26 assessments and March 29–April 2 break; finish April 5–8.

**Submit by Apr 8** An AI-assisted Google Doc or Sheet (or a notebook section), your prompt, two independent checks and a short critique.

**Check understanding** Show the original AI output and your corrections. Do not assume AI opened the URL: ask what rows and columns it actually read. Use only the public teaching dataset.

Use the free, school-approved AI tool available to the class. Export to Docs or Sheets only if enabled. Otherwise paste the output into a Doc or notebook and label it AI-generated. If student AI access is unavailable, use a teacher-provided response. No paid plan is required by this lesson.
